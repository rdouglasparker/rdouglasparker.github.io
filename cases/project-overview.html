How this project works

Over the semester your group will serve as the Financial Reporting Advisory Team for Catamount Ridge Gear, Inc. ("CRG"), a Western North Carolina outdoor-gear manufacturer. CRG's leadership is closing the 2026 fiscal year and planning for 2027. In each of four cases, management faces real choices about how to structure transactions and how to report them. Your job is to lay out the alternatives, run the numbers, take a position, and defend it.

There is rarely a single "textbook" answer. The right answer depends on the facts, and the facts are different for each version of the case.

Company profile

Catamount Ridge Gear, Inc. was founded in a Cullowhee garage by two Western Carolina graduates who were tired of backpack straps that failed halfway up Whiteside Mountain. What started as a repair business grew into a designer and manufacturer of technical outdoor equipment. CRG's headquarters is in Sylva, North Carolina, and its main manufacturing plant sits along the Tuckasegee River in Dillsboro.

Product lines

  • Packs and bags — the original "Ridgeline" daypacks and multi-day packs (about 40% of revenue).
  • Shelter — "Balsam" tents and hammock systems (about 25%).
  • Water — "Fontana" inflatable and hard-shell paddleboards (about 20%).
  • Trail tools — "Summit Lock" trekking poles and accessories (about 15%).

Ownership and financing

CRG's common stock trades on a national stock exchange. About 2,000,000 common shares are outstanding, and founders and employees together own roughly 30% of them. CRG borrows from Blue Ridge Mountain Bank under a revolving line of credit and term notes, and it has publicly issued bonds outstanding. Its lending agreements contain financial covenants, and these matter in several cases.

Remember: management's preference is not the standard

Executives in these cases will tell you what they would like the answer to be. Sometimes they are right. Sometimes they are not. Your memo must rest on the facts and on U.S. GAAP (the FASB Accounting Standards Codification), not on what produces the most attractive numbers.

Versions

Each case comes in three versions. The company and the questions are the same, but key facts (ownership percentages, interest rates, lease terms, legal opinions, stock prices, and so on) change. Because of this, a well-supported answer for one version may be the wrong answer for another.

  • Your group is assigned one version for the entire series. Facts carry forward from case to case within your version.
  • Do not rely on another group's conclusions. Comparing versions after submission is encouraged, and we will debrief the differences in class.

What every case asks you to do

  1. Identify the issues. What decisions does CRG face, and what accounting questions follow from them?
  2. Lay out the alternatives. For each issue, describe the realistic options, including the one management prefers.
  3. Run the numbers. Prepare the journal entries, schedules, and financial statement effects under each alternative the case asks about.
  4. Take a position. Recommend a treatment or course of action and explain why it is required or preferable.
  5. Support it. Cite the relevant FASB Codification topic and connect the facts of your version to the criteria. Address the strongest counterargument.

Group deliverables for each case

  • Memo to the CFO (no more than 4 pages, single-spaced, excluding exhibits) that states each issue, the alternatives, your recommendation, and your support.
  • Exhibits: journal entries, calculations, schedules, and any statement excerpts. Label each exhibit and refer to it in the memo.
  • Team record: a short list of who contributed what, and any point where your group disagreed and how you resolved it.

Submit one set of deliverables per group. Follow the due dates posted in Canvas.

Ground rules

  • Assume a combined income tax rate of 25% unless a case says otherwise.
  • Round to the nearest dollar unless a case says amounts are in thousands.
  • Show your work. A correct number with no support earns partial credit at best.
  • When the facts are incomplete, state a reasonable assumption and explain how a different assumption would change your answer.
