Case 3: Taxes, Shareholders, and Stock-Based Pay

Deferred taxes, returning capital, executive compensation, and earnings per share

Deferred taxes, the 2027 capital plan, and the executive pay proposal

Three items need your team's analysis before the board's planning meeting. The first affects the 2026 statements we are about to finalize. The second and third are decisions the board will make for 2027, and the board wants to understand how each option will affect reported earnings per share.

Issue 1: Is CRG's deferred tax asset realizable?

At December 31, 2026, CRG has $4,000,000 of deductible temporary differences (accrued warranty, recall, and other costs that are deductible for tax only when paid). The balance was the same at the beginning of the year. Using the 25% tax rate, the related deferred tax asset is $1,000,000.

During 2026, taxable temporary differences from accelerated tax depreciation increased by $600,000, so the deferred tax liability increased by $150,000.

Other evidence

  • CRG has reported pretax income in each of the last eight years.
  • Orders on hand for 2027 are 12% higher than at this time last year.
  • The deductible temporary differences relate to warranty and other accrued costs that are expected to reverse (become deductible) over the next two years, when CRG expects to have ample taxable income.
"Let's set up a $300,000 valuation allowance anyway. It's conservative, the auditors never complain about conservative, and it gives us something to release if next year is tight."— Marisa Holloway, CFO

Issue 2: Returning capital to shareholders

The board has set aside $3,000,000 of cash to return to shareholders on July 1, 2027. Common stock has a $1 par value. 2,000,000 common shares ($1 par) are outstanding at January 1, 2027.

Three proposals are on the table:

Proposal A — Cash dividend. Declare and pay cash dividends totaling $3,000,000.
Proposal B — Share repurchase. Use the cash to buy back common shares on the open market on July 1 at the expected market price. CRG would hold the shares as treasury stock (cost method).
Proposal C — Stock dividend. Keep the cash for operations and instead distribute a 10% stock dividend on July 1.

"I don't care which one we do, as long as we hit the consensus number. Missing by even a penny costs us 10% on the stock price."— Walt Cardinal, CEO

Issue 3: The executive pay proposal

The compensation committee will grant one of two awards to CRG's six senior executives on January 1, 2027. Either award cliff-vests after four years of continued service. CRG accounts for forfeitures when they occur, and no forfeitures are expected.

Plan X — Stock options. 120,000 nonqualified options with an exercise price equal to the January 1 market price ($40.00). An option-pricing model estimates the fair value at $12.00 per option on the grant date.
Plan Y — Restricted stock units (RSUs). 40,000 RSUs, each settled in one common share when it vests.

The expected average market price of CRG's common stock during 2027 is $46.00. Use the 25% tax rate for the after-tax effect of compensation expense. Assume the number of options or RSUs is not adjusted for any stock dividend in Issue 2.

"Options are the better deal for shareholders. They barely touch diluted EPS and cost less to expense than RSUs."— Marisa Holloway, CFO

Questions for your group

Issue 1

  1. What standard must be met to report a deferred tax asset without a valuation allowance? List the positive and negative evidence in your version and weigh it. Which evidence is the most objective and verifiable?
  2. What valuation allowance, if any, should CRG report at December 31, 2026? Prepare the 2026 income tax journal entry or entries.
  3. Compute 2026 income tax expense and net income under your recommendation and under management's proposal. Respond to management's position.

Issue 2

  1. Prepare the July 1 journal entry for each proposal. For Proposal C, explain whether the stock dividend is "small" or "large" and why that matters.
  2. Compute projected 2027 basic EPS under each proposal. Show the numerator and weighted-average shares.
  3. Which proposals are legally and contractually permitted? Consider the bond covenant.
  4. Which proposal do you recommend? Address the CEO's focus on the consensus estimate. Is it appropriate to choose a capital-return method to hit an EPS target?

Issue 3

  1. Compute total compensation cost and 2027 compensation expense under each plan. Prepare the 2027 entry for each.
  2. Using the treasury stock method, compute the incremental shares each plan would add to 2027 diluted EPS. Is either plan antidilutive? Explain why.
  3. Combine your Issue 2 recommendation with each plan: what are projected 2027 basic and diluted EPS?
  4. Which plan do you recommend? Respond to the executive's comment and discuss incentives, retention, and dilution in light of your version's stock price outlook.
