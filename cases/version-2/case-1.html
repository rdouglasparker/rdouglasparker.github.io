Issue 1: Investments

1A. Corporate bond portfolio

On July 1, 2026, CRG paid $2,000,000 (face value) for high-grade corporate bonds that pay 5% annual interest each June 30 and December 31 and mature in 2035. CRG received the December 31 interest payment. The bonds' fair value at December 31, 2026, was $1,950,000. The issuer's credit rating has not changed, and no credit losses are expected.

CRG's board approved a written investment policy stating that these bonds will be held until they mature in 2035. CRG's cash forecasts show no need to sell them, and the company has an unused $8 million line of credit for liquidity needs.

"Put them in available-for-sale. I like having the flexibility to sell if rates move, and I don't want to be locked in."— Marisa Holloway, CFO

1B. Stake in Tuckasegee Textile Mills

Tuckasegee Textile Mills, Inc. ("TTM") is a fabric mill in Bryson City that makes the coated nylon CRG uses in its packs and tents. TTM has 400,000 common shares outstanding, which trade on an over-the-counter market with a readily determinable fair value.

On January 2, 2026, CRG purchased 92,000 shares of TTM at $20.00 per share. Other facts:

  • Haywood Holdings LLC owns 62% of TTM's common stock and controls its board.
  • CRG formally requested a board seat in March; Haywood Holdings voted down the request.
  • TTM's management has declined CRG's requests for internal financial information beyond what it gives all shareholders.
  • TTM supplies about 15% of CRG's fabric under an ordinary arm's-length purchase agreement; there is no exchange of managers or technical staff.

Issue 2: The maturing bank note

CRG owes Blue Ridge Mountain Bank a $5,000,000 note payable that matures on March 31, 2027. Blue Ridge's loan agreement includes a covenant requiring CRG to maintain a current ratio of at least 1.25 at every fiscal year-end. A violation allows the bank to demand immediate repayment of all of CRG's borrowings from the bank.

At December 31, 2026, CRG has current assets of $18,000,000 and current liabilities, not counting this note, of $10,000,000.

CRG has been negotiating with Blue Ridge Mountain Bank to refinance the note into a five-year term loan. On February 15, 2027, the bank issued a nonbinding letter of intent stating it is "favorably disposed" toward the refinancing. The bank's credit committee will not vote on the loan until April 2027, after the note's March 31 maturity date. Management expects to repay the note from operating cash if the refinancing does not happen.

"The bank is going to do this deal. Everyone knows it. Classify the note as long-term."— Marisa Holloway, CFO

Issue 3: The Summit Lock lawsuit

In August 2026, a group of customers filed suit against CRG, alleging that a locking collar on certain Summit Lock trekking poles slipped under load and caused falls. CRG stopped selling the affected model in September.

Outside counsel's letter dated February 18, 2027, states that an unfavorable outcome is reasonably possible but not probable. If CRG loses, counsel estimates the loss would fall between $400,000 and $900,000.

Separately, CRG has sued the supplier of the locking collar. CRG's attorneys believe it is probable that CRG will recover about $250,000 from the supplier, though the supplier is contesting the claim.

"Let's be conservative and accrue the full $900,000 now. We had a strong year, and if we win, we can reverse it next year when we might need the earnings."— Marisa Holloway, CFO
"And if we're likely to collect $250,000 from the supplier, shouldn't we book that, too? It offsets the lawsuit."— Walt Cardinal, CEO

Questions for your group

Issue 1A

  1. What are the possible classifications for the bond investment? What does each require, and how would each affect CRG's 2026 net income, other comprehensive income, and balance sheet?
  2. Which classification is appropriate for your version's facts? Is more than one acceptable? Respond directly to the CFO's preference.
  3. Prepare the December 31, 2026, entries for the interest received and any fair value adjustment under your recommended classification.

Issue 1B

  1. Does CRG have significant influence over TTM? Explain using the ownership level and the qualitative factors in your version. Is the 20% guideline a rule or a presumption?
  2. Prepare a side-by-side comparison of the equity method and fair value through net income: 2026 journal entries, income reported, and December 31 carrying amount.
  3. Which method do you recommend? Respond directly to the CFO's argument.

Issue 2

  1. How should the $5,000,000 note be classified on the December 31, 2026, balance sheet? Identify the conditions that must be met to exclude a short-term obligation from current liabilities and apply each to your version.
  2. Compute the current ratio under management's proposed classification and under your recommendation. Is CRG in compliance with the covenant? If not, what are the consequences, and what should management do before the statements are issued?

Issue 3

  1. Should CRG accrue a loss for the lawsuit, disclose it only, or both? If you accrue, what amount, and why? Prepare any entry.
  2. Draft the disclosure language you would include in the notes (three to five sentences).
  3. How should the $250,000 expected recovery from the supplier be reported? Respond to the CEO.
  4. Ethics: identify one point in this case where management's preference would make the statements less faithful to the facts. What would you say to the CFO, and what would you do if she insisted?
