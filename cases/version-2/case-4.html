All amounts in this case are in thousands of dollars. Use a 25% tax rate. CRG presents comparative statements for 2026 and 2025.

Preliminary 2026 net income, before the three items below, is $4,150. Net income as originally reported for 2025 was $1,500.

Part 1: Three year-end discoveries

Item a: Depreciation method

Molding equipment was purchased on January 1, 2023, for $3,600 and has been depreciated straight-line over 10 years with no residual value. Starting in 2026, CRG will use the double-declining-balance method for this equipment because new evidence shows its productivity drops sharply in later years. The remaining life is unchanged. Preliminary 2026 net income includes $360 of straight-line depreciation on this equipment.

Item b: Prior-year inventory count

While reviewing the 2025 physical count, Priya found that a pallet of tents was counted twice. Ending inventory at December 31, 2025, was overstated by $120. CRG uses a periodic system, and the 2026 books have not been closed. The error also affected the 2025 tax return; CRG will file an amended return.

Item c: Bad debt estimate

Two of CRG's specialty-store customers filed for bankruptcy this fall. Based on updated aging analysis, management will raise its bad debt estimate from 2% to 3% of credit sales, beginning with 2026. 2026 credit sales were $30,000. Preliminary 2026 net income used 2%.

"Can't we just run all of this through this year's income statement and move on? Restating last year makes us look sloppy."— Marisa Holloway, CFO

Blue Ridge Mountain Bank's loan agreement requires cash flows from operating activities of at least $5,500 for 2026. Falling short is an event of default unless the bank grants a waiver.

The CFO's proposals

Marisa has suggested three ways to strengthen operating cash flow. Treat each as if it could still be done on or before December 31, 2026, and evaluate it.

Proposal 1 — Factor receivables. Sell $600 of customer receivables on December 28 to a factoring company without recourse for a 2% fee. The transfer would qualify as a sale.
Proposal 2 — Reclassify interest. Report the $750 of interest paid during 2026 as a financing activity, "since interest is a cost of financing."
Proposal 3 — Stretch payables. Hold $1,200 of supplier payments due December 28–31 until January 5. Most of the invoices are from TTM; the payments would be past the due dates and would incur late fees.

Questions for your group

Part 1

  1. For each item, identify the type of accounting change or error and the reporting approach (retrospective, prospective, or restatement with a prior period adjustment). Cite the relevant guidance.
  2. Prepare any journal entries needed in 2026 for each item. Show the effect on 2026 pretax income and on retained earnings at January 1, 2026.
  3. For any item reported retrospectively or as a restatement, show how 2025 net income and the January 1, 2025, retained earnings balance would change in the comparative statements.
  4. Reconcile preliminary 2026 net income ($4,150) to the final net income given in Part 2 ($3,745).
  5. For any error, is a full reissuance of prior statements ("Big R" restatement) required, or would correcting the comparative amounts ("little r" revision) be acceptable? Discuss quantitative and qualitative materiality. Respond to the CFO.
  6. Draft the key points of the required note disclosure for one of the three items.

Part 2

  1. Prepare the operating activities section of CRG's 2026 statement of cash flows using the indirect method.
  2. Does CRG meet the $5,500 covenant? By how much is it over or under?
  3. Classify each transaction in Table 3 as operating, investing, financing, noncash (disclosed separately), or not reported in the 2026 statement. Explain any that are judgment calls.
  4. Evaluate each of the CFO's three proposals: is it permitted under GAAP, what would it do to operating cash flow, and is it a sound business decision? What do you recommend CRG do about the covenant?
  5. Looking ahead: based on your group's Case 2 recommendation, explain how the Dillsboro Line financing will appear in CRG's 2027 statement of cash flows.
