Part 1

Revenue, cost of construction, and gross profit

The contract with the National Park Service does not qualify for revenue recognition over time, so Fontana Bridge Works recognizes revenue and cost of construction only when the bridge is completed. A loss on the entire project cannot wait, however: when the latest estimates show that total costs will exceed the contract price, the entire expected loss must be recognized in the first year that loss is anticipated, even though the bridge is not yet complete. Cost of construction is the contract’s equivalent of cost of goods sold.

a.Calculate the amount of revenue, cost of construction, and gross profit (loss) to be recognized in each of the three years, and enter the three-year totals. Enter 0 where nothing is recognized.
ItemTotal

In the year the revised estimates first show total costs exceeding the contract price, the loss on long-term contracts = (cumulative costs incurred to date + estimated costs to complete) − contract price. The loss is recognized in full at once and reduces CIP; no revenue or cost of construction is recognized until the bridge is completed.

At completion, revenue is the contract price and cost of construction is the total of all construction costs incurred less the loss already recognized, because that loss reduced CIP. The gross profit (loss) row combines revenue, cost of construction, and any loss on long-term contracts recognized that year, so its three-year total equals the contract price less the total costs actually incurred.

Part 2

Journal entries

Record every entry for each of the three years. Construction costs are debited to Construction in progress (CIP), the contractor's work-in-process account. Progress billings create an account receivable and are credited to Billings on construction contract. The entry that recognizes revenue and cost of construction also adds the gross profit to CIP (or subtracts a loss from it). When the bridge is completed in the final year, close out the billings and CIP accounts. When a loss on the entire project is anticipated, record it in the year it is first anticipated by debiting Loss on long-term contracts and crediting CIP; the entry at completion then reflects only what remains to be recognized.
