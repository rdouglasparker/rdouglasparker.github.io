The pattern used throughout: inventory is never reported above what it is worth. FIFO and average-cost inventory uses lower of cost or NRV; LIFO and retail-method inventory uses lower of cost or market, where market is the middle of replacement cost, NRV, and NRV − normal profit.

The company used in Problems 1–4

Pinnacle Park Sports sells outdoor gear from a store in Sylva. Its fiscal year ends December 31.

  • The main store uses FIFO. At December 31, 2026: bikes cost $80,000 and will sell for $96,000 with $12,000 of costs to sell; kayaks cost $54,000 and will sell for $58,000 with $7,000 of costs to sell; helmets cost $16,000 and will sell for $17,500 with $2,500 of costs to sell. Pinnacle Park applies the test item by item and includes write-downs in cost of goods sold.
  • The ski shop uses LIFO (details in Problem 3).
  • In October 2026, Pinnacle Park signed a noncancelable contract to buy bikes for $250,000 in March 2027.

Explanation

LIFO inventory uses lower of cost or market. For boots, the ceiling is NRV, $140 − $10 = $130; the floor is $130 − $20 = $110. Replacement cost ($104) is below the floor, so designated market is the middle value, $110. Since $110 is below cost, write down $10 × 400 = $4,000.

For snowboards, the ceiling is $190 and the floor is $160. Replacement cost ($215) is above the ceiling, so market is $190. Write down ($200 − $190) × 150 = $1,500. Total write-down = $5,500.
