ACCT 351 · Revenue Recognition — Chapter 6, Part C

Long-Term Contracts

Guided Walk-Through

Both walk-throughs below use the same setup: at the start of 2024, Harding Construction Company signs a $5,000,000 contract to build an office building. The project is expected to take three years, revenue qualifies for recognition over time, and progress is measured with the cost-to-cost ratio. Work each year yourself before opening the reveal — that's where the learning happens.

2024Year One

Your turn — before you open the reveal
  1. Calculate the percentage of completion at the end of 2024.
  2. Calculate cumulative revenue to be recognized to date, and revenue recognized this year.
  3. Calculate cost of construction and gross profit for 2024.
Reveal the calculation

% complete = $1,500,000 ÷ $3,750,000 = 40%

Cumulative revenue to date = $5,000,000 × 40% = $2,000,000. Since no revenue was recognized in a prior year, revenue recognized in 2024 = $2,000,000.

Cost of construction = actual costs incurred = $1,500,000

Gross profit = $2,000,000 − $1,500,000 = $500,000

2025Year Two — the loss shows up

Your turn — this one's harder
  1. Is the entire contract now expected to be profitable or unprofitable? Compare the new total estimated cost to the $5,000,000 contract price.
  2. Revenue is still recognized normally with the cost-to-cost ratio — calculate the new cumulative % complete and revenue recognized in 2025.
  3. Because this is now an overall-loss contract, cumulative gross profit (loss) recognized to date must equal the full estimated contract loss. Since $500,000 of profit was already recognized in 2024, what loss must be recognized in 2025 to bring the cumulative total down to the full loss?
  4. Use the plug formula (Cost of Construction = Revenue recognized + Loss recognized) to back into Cost of Construction for 2025.
Reveal the calculation

Step 1 — is the whole contract now a loser? Total estimated cost is now $5,100,000 against a $5,000,000 contract price → the entire project is now expected to lose $100,000.

Step 2 — revenue, as usual: % complete = $2,760,000 ÷ $5,100,000 = 54.12%. Cumulative revenue to date = $5,000,000 × 54.12% = $2,706,000. Less 2024's $2,000,000 → revenue recognized in 2025 = $706,000.

Step 3 — the loss needed this period: cumulative gross profit (loss) to date must equal the full $(100,000) loss. 2024 already booked +$500,000, so 2025 must record a loss of $500,000 + $100,000 = $(600,000).

Step 4 — plug Cost of Construction: Cost of Construction = Revenue recognized + Loss recognized = $706,000 + $600,000 = $1,306,000 (notice this is not the same as the $1,260,000 actually spent this year — that's the whole point of the plug).

Reveal the 2025 journal entries
Construction in Progress (CIP)1,260,000
Cash, Materials, etc.1,260,000

To record construction costs (actual cash cost incurred — note this differs from Cost of Construction below).

Accounts Receivable2,000,000
Billings on Construction Contract2,000,000

To record progress billings.

Cash1,400,000
Accounts Receivable1,400,000

To record cash collections.

Cost of Construction1,306,000
Revenue from Long-Term Contracts706,000
Construction in Progress (CIP)600,000

To record revenue and gross loss. Notice CIP is credited this time — the loss reduces CIP instead of adding to it, keeping CIP from being carried at more than the project is actually worth.

2026Year Three — Completion

Your turn
  1. Calculate revenue recognized in the final year (% complete is now 100%).
  2. The total project loss is now $5,000,000 − $5,200,000 = $(200,000). Cumulative loss to date after 2024–2025 is $(100,000) — what loss must 2026 record to reach $(200,000)?
  3. Plug Cost of Construction for 2026.
Reveal the calculation

Cumulative revenue to date = $5,000,000 × 100% = $5,000,000. Less revenue recognized in 2024–2025 ($2,706,000) → revenue recognized in 2026 = $2,294,000.

Cumulative loss must now total $(200,000) [$5,000,000 contract price − $5,200,000 actual total cost]. Since $(100,000) was already recognized in 2025 (net of 2024's profit), 2026 must record an additional loss of $(100,000).

Cost of Construction = Revenue recognized + Loss recognized = $2,294,000 + $100,000 = $2,394,000.

Three-year summary check: Revenue $2,000,000 + $706,000 + $2,294,000 = $5,000,000 (still the full contract price — revenue recognition doesn't change just because the project loses money). Cost of Construction $1,500,000 + $1,306,000 + $2,394,000 = $5,200,000 (matches actual total cost). Gross profit (loss) $500,000 − $600,000 − $100,000 = $(200,000) total project loss.

Why did 2024 "get it wrong"? Recognizing revenue over time means early estimates drive early profit recognition. When 2025's cost revision revealed the project was now a loser, that $500,000 of 2024 profit had to be reversed — plus the full $100,000 estimated loss — all in 2025. This is treated as a change in accounting estimate, so 2024's financial statements are never restated.
