Lower of Cost or Net Realizable Value

LCNRV

Example 1 — Single Product

Sapphire Valley Outfitters carries a hiking tent in ending inventory. Cost is $24 per unit, selling price is $35 per unit, and estimated selling costs are $5 per unit. What unit value should Sapphire Valley use for this item when applying LCNRV?

$
Solution

NRV = Selling price − Selling costs = $35 − $5 = $30.
Cost ($24) is lower than NRV ($30), so no write-down is needed — the item is reported at its already-recorded cost.

LCNRV unit value = $24

c) By total inventory — what total amount should be reported for ending inventory?

$
Solution

Total cost = $332,000; total NRV = $345,000. Since total cost is lower, no write-down is needed at all.

Notice the pattern: $316,000 (individual) < $325,000 (category) < $332,000 (total). Grouping items together always lets increases in some items offset decreases in others, so a broader level of aggregation never produces a lower reported inventory value.
Lower of Cost or Market

LCM (Ceiling and Floor)

Example 1 — Replacement Cost Between the Ceiling and Floor

Balsam Ridge Distributors uses LIFO and reports inventory at LCM. One product has a year-end selling price of $96,000, estimated selling costs of $12,000, and a normal profit margin of 20% of selling price. Replacement cost is $70,000, and recorded cost is $65,000. What amount should Balsam Ridge report for this item?

$
Solution

Ceiling (NRV) = $96,000 − $12,000 = $84,000.
Normal profit = $96,000 × 20% = $19,200.
Floor (NRV − normal profit) = $84,000 − $19,200 = $64,800.
Replacement cost ($70,000) falls between the floor and the ceiling, so market = replacement cost = $70,000.
Compare market ($70,000) to cost ($65,000) — cost is lower.

LCM value = $65,000

Example 2 — Replacement Cost Above the Ceiling

A second product at Balsam Ridge has a selling price of $50,000, estimated selling costs of $5,000, and a normal profit margin of 25% of selling price. Replacement cost is $54,000, and recorded cost is $40,000. What amount should Balsam Ridge report for this item?

$
Solution

Ceiling (NRV) = $50,000 − $5,000 = $45,000.
Normal profit = $50,000 × 25% = $12,500.
Floor = $45,000 − $12,500 = $32,500.
Replacement cost ($54,000) is above the ceiling, so market = the ceiling = $45,000.
Compare market ($45,000) to cost ($40,000) — cost is lower.

LCM value = $40,000

Estimating Inventory

The Gross Profit Method

Example 1 — Estimating a Loss

On March 14, , a fire destroyed the entire inventory in a warehouse owned by Maggie Valley Mercantile. The company's periodic accounting records show beginning inventory of $84,000, net purchases of $210,000, and net sales of $360,000 through the date of the fire. The gross profit ratio has averaged 35% over the past three years. Estimate the cost of the inventory destroyed.

$
Solution

Goods available for sale = $84,000 + $210,000 = $294,000.
Estimated COGS = $360,000 × (1 − 0.35) = $234,000.
Estimated ending inventory (destroyed) = $294,000 − $234,000 = $60,000

Example 2 — Solving for the Gross Profit Ratio

Cashiers Creek Supply estimates that $60,000 of inventory was lost in a flood in . Its periodic records show beginning inventory of $70,000, net purchases of $200,000, and net sales of $300,000 for the period. What is the company's gross profit ratio? (Enter as a percentage, e.g., enter 35 for 35%.)

%
Solution

Goods available for sale = $70,000 + $200,000 = $270,000.
Estimated COGS = Goods available − Ending inventory (lost) = $270,000 − $60,000 = $210,000.
Estimated gross profit = Sales − COGS = $300,000 − $210,000 = $90,000.
Gross profit ratio = $90,000 ÷ $300,000 = 30%

Retail Inventory Method

Approximating Average Cost

Dillsboro Dry Goods uses a periodic inventory system and the retail inventory method. The following data are available for the month of October, :

CostRetail
Beginning inventory$84,000$140,000
Net purchases258,240410,000
Net markups8,000
Net markdowns6,000
Net sales378,000
Example 1 — Cost-to-Retail Percentage

Using the average cost approach (net markups and net markdowns both included when calculating the percentage), what is the cost-to-retail percentage? (Enter as a percentage, e.g., enter 55 for 55%.)

%
Solution

Goods available at cost = $84,000 + $258,240 = $342,240.
Goods available at retail = $140,000 + $410,000 + $8,000 − $6,000 = $552,000.
Cost-to-retail % = $342,240 ÷ $552,000 = 62%

Example 2 — Estimated Ending Inventory at Cost

Using the 62% cost-to-retail percentage from Example 1, what is the estimated ending inventory at cost?

$
Solution

Ending inventory at retail = $552,000 (goods available at retail) − $378,000 (net sales) = $174,000.
Ending inventory at cost = $174,000 × 62% = $107,880
(Estimated cost of goods sold = $342,240 − $107,880 = $234,360.)

Retail Inventory Method

The Conventional Method (Approximating Lower of Average Cost or Market)

Using the same October data for Dillsboro Dry Goods, now apply the conventional retail method, which excludes net markdowns from the cost-to-retail percentage calculation (markdowns are subtracted only after the percentage is calculated).

Example 1 — Conventional Cost-to-Retail Percentage

What is the cost-to-retail percentage under the conventional method? (Enter as a percentage, rounded to two decimals — e.g., enter 55.25 for 55.25%.)

%
Solution

Goods available at cost (unchanged) = $342,240.
Goods available at retail, excluding net markdowns = $140,000 + $410,000 + $8,000 = $558,000.
Cost-to-retail % = $342,240 ÷ $558,000 = 61.33% (rounded)
Notice this is lower than the 62% average-cost percentage from the previous section — excluding markdowns from the denominator always produces a lower (more conservative) percentage whenever markdowns exist.

Example 2 — Estimated Cost of Goods Sold

Now subtract net markdowns and net sales to find ending inventory at retail, then apply the 61.33% conventional percentage. What is the estimated cost of goods sold for October?

$
Solution

Goods available at retail (including markdowns) = $558,000 − $6,000 = $552,000.
Ending inventory at retail = $552,000 − $378,000 (net sales) = $174,000 — the same retail-value figure as the average cost method; only the percentage applied to it changes.
Ending inventory at cost = $174,000 × 61.33% ≈ $106,720.
Estimated cost of goods sold = $342,240 − $106,720 = $235,520
