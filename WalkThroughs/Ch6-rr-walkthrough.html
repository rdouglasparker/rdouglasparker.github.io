Key idea: Revenue is recognized when (or as) a seller satisfies a performance obligation by transferring control of a promised good or service. The five steps: (1) identify the contract, (2) identify the performance obligations, (3) determine the transaction price, (4) allocate the transaction price to the performance obligations, and (5) recognize revenue when (or as) each performance obligation is satisfied.

The company used in Problems 1–4

Catamount Interactive makes the Ridgeline gaming console and Ridgeline Online, a 12-month subscription to its online gaming service. In every problem, Catamount sells 1,000 units to Sylva Electronics, a retailer, on January 1, 2026. Only the contract terms change.

Explanation

This meets the first criterion for recognition over time: subscribers receive and consume the benefit of access continuously as Catamount provides it, day by day, over the year.

On January 1, Catamount has been paid but hasn't yet provided any access, so no revenue is recognized. The $120,000 received is a liability (deferred revenue) representing Catamount's obligation to provide access for the year.

$120,000 ÷ 12 = $10,000 per month

After January's entry, deferred revenue is $120,000 − $10,000 = $110,000.

Explanation

Step 2, performance obligations: the console and the subscription are each capable of being distinct (each is sold on its own) and separately identifiable (neither significantly modifies the other). So the contract has two performance obligations: (1) delivery of the console and (2) one year of subscription access.

Step 4, relative stand-alone selling prices: total stand-alone value = $280 + $120 = $400. Console share = $280 ÷ $400 = 70%. Subscription share = $120 ÷ $400 = 30%.

Compare all three contracts: the same $280 console and $120 subscription produce different revenue depending on how they're sold. Sold separately, the consoles produce $280,000 at delivery (Problem 1) and the subscriptions produce $10,000 per month (Problem 2). Bundled for $360, the same items produce $252,000 at delivery and $9,000 per month. The price allocation, not the cost of providing the goods or services, drives the difference.
