LO8-1

Perpetual vs. Periodic — Cost of Goods Sold

Beginning inventory + Net purchases − Ending inventory = Cost of goods sold.

Example 1

A company uses a periodic inventory system. Beginning inventory was $62,000. Net purchases for the year (including freight, net of returns and discounts) totaled $348,000. A physical count at year-end showed $71,000 of inventory on hand. What is cost of goods sold for the year?

$
Solution

$62,000 + $348,000 − $71,000 = $339,000

Example 2

A different company reports beginning inventory of $115,000 and ending inventory of $98,000. Cost of goods sold for the year was $520,000. What were net purchases for the year?

$
Solution

Rearranging the formula: Net purchases = COGS − Beginning inventory + Ending inventory
$520,000 − $115,000 + $98,000 = $503,000

LO8-2

Which Goods Belong in Inventory?

Example 1 — Goods in Transit

Cataloochee Supply's physical count of its warehouse on December 31 totaled $210,000. That count did not include either of these two shipments:

  • $14,000 of goods Cataloochee shipped to a customer on December 29, terms F.O.B. shipping point (customer received them January 3).
  • $9,000 of goods Cataloochee purchased from a supplier, shipped December 30, terms F.O.B. shipping point (Cataloochee received them January 4).

What amount should Cataloochee report as ending inventory?

$
Solution

The goods Cataloochee sold F.O.B. shipping point transferred title at shipment — they were correctly excluded from the count (they're the buyer's now). The goods Cataloochee purchased F.O.B. shipping point also transferred title at shipment, meaning Cataloochee owned them from December 30 even though they hadn't arrived — those $9,000 must be added to the physical count.

$210,000 + $9,000 = $219,000

Example 2 — Consignment

Deep Creek Hardware's physical count came to $186,000, which included $22,000 of inventory held on consignment from a supplier. What should Deep Creek report as its own ending inventory?

$
Solution

$186,000 − $22,000 = $164,000. Consigned goods belong to the consignor until sold — Deep Creek never owned them, so they must be removed even though they were on Deep Creek's shelves during the count.

LO8-3

Purchase Discounts — Gross vs. Net Method

Example 1 — Gross Method

A company purchases $48,000 of inventory, terms 2/10, n/30, and uses the gross method. It pays the full balance within the discount period. How much cash does it pay?

$
Solution

Discount = $48,000 × 2% = $960. Cash paid = $48,000 − $960 = $47,040

Accounts payable48,000
Inventory960
Cash47,040
Example 2 — Net Method

A company purchases $60,000 of inventory, terms 1/10, n/30, and uses the net method (recording the purchase net of the discount). It pays the full balance after the discount period has passed. What amount does it record as Purchase Discounts Lost?

$
Solution

At purchase, the company recorded Inventory/Accounts payable at $60,000 × 99% = $59,400 — already net of the 1% discount. Since the discount period lapsed, the company must now recognize the $600 discount it assumed but didn't earn.

Accounts payable59,400
Purchase discounts lost600
Cash60,000

Purchase discounts lost = $600
