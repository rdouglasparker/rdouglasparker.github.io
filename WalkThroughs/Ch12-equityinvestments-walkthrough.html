  1. Fair value through net income. Below 20% with no other sign of influence, the investor is presumed to lack significant influence.
  2. Equity method. Ownership of 20% to 50% gives rise to a presumption of significant influence.
  3. Consolidation. More than 50% of the voting shares means control. The parent and subsidiary report combined (consolidated) financial statements.

Significant Influence: The Equity Method

Facts for Problems 5–10

On January 2, 2026, Jackson Ridge pays $2,400,000 in cash for 30% of the common stock of Balsam Valley Brewing Co. and gains significant influence. At that date:

  • Fair values exceed book values for buildings by $900,000, for land by $300,000, and for inventory by $200,000. Book value equals fair value for all other items.

Problem 13 — Changing Methods

The level of Jackson Ridge's influence over an investee changes. How should it account for the change?

Your answers vs. the answers
Your answers compared with the correct answers for Problem 13
ItemYour answerAnswer
Explanation

(a) Equity method to another method: no adjustment is made to the remaining carrying amount. The equity method is simply discontinued. The balance in the investment account becomes the new cost basis, and from then on the investment is written up or down to fair value.

(b) Another method to the equity method: the existing shares are first marked to fair value on the date of the change, with any unrealized gain or loss recognized in net income. That fair value (plus the cost of any additional shares) becomes the starting balance in the equity-method investment account. There's no retroactive restatement. A disclosure note describes the change.
