Multiple Temporary and Permanent Differences

Sort permanent from temporary differences, then measure deferred tax assets and liabilities together.

Two kinds of differences: Temporary differences reverse over time and create deferred tax liabilities (future taxable amounts) or deferred tax assets (future deductible amounts). Permanent differences never reverse, so they change taxable income and tax payable but create no deferred taxes. They're the reason a company's effective tax rate differs from the statutory rate.

The company used in all problems

Ramsey Center Robotics, Inc. reports pretax accounting income of $800,000 for 2026. Its accountants identified the following items. The enacted tax rate is 25% for all years, and Ramsey Center had no deferred tax balances at the start of 2026.

  1. Municipal bond interest of $20,000 is included in pretax accounting income. Interest on municipal bonds is never taxable.
  2. Life insurance premiums of $12,000 on policies covering company officers (Ramsey Center is the beneficiary) were expensed. These premiums are never tax deductible.
  3. Depreciation on the tax return exceeds depreciation in the income statement by $40,000 in 2026. The difference will reverse in 2027 ($15,000) and 2028 ($25,000).
  4. Estimated warranty expense of $30,000 was accrued in 2026; nothing has been paid yet. Warranty costs are deductible when paid, expected to be $18,000 in 2027 and $12,000 in 2028.
  5. Rent received in advance: On December 1, 2026, Ramsey Center collected $24,000 of rent for space it subleases, covering all of 2027. The rent is taxable when received but will be reported as revenue in the income statement in 2027.

Identifying the Differences

Problem 1 — Sort the Differences

Classify each item. For temporary differences, decide whether the item creates future taxable amounts or future deductible amounts.

Explanation

  1. Permanent. Municipal bond interest is in the income statement but will never be on a tax return. It doesn't reverse, so it creates no deferred taxes.
  2. Permanent. The premiums are an expense in the income statement but will never be deductible. Again, nothing reverses.
  3. Future taxable amounts. Taking more depreciation on the tax return now means less later, so taxable income will be higher in 2027 and 2028. This creates a deferred tax liability.
  4. Future deductible amounts. The warranty expense is reported now but deducted when paid in 2027 and 2028. This creates a deferred tax asset.
  5. Future deductible amounts. The rent is taxed now, but it will be reported as revenue in 2027 without being taxed again. In effect, it lowers 2027 taxable income relative to accounting income. This creates a deferred tax asset, related to the deferred rent revenue liability in the balance sheet.

Test for a temporary difference: will this item eventually show up in both pretax accounting income and taxable income, just in different years? If yes, it's temporary. If it will only ever appear in one of them, it's permanent.

Deferred tax assets and deferred tax liabilities are always classified as noncurrent. When they relate to the same taxpaying component and the same tax jurisdiction, they're netted and shown as a single amount:

$13,500 DTA − $10,000 DTL = $3,500 net noncurrent deferred tax asset

The $201,500 income tax payable is still reported separately as a current liability.
