A company promotes its products in ways that obligate it to do something in the future. Match each promotion to the appropriate accounting.

Your answers vs. the answers
Your answers compared with the correct answers for Problem 8
ItemYour answerAnswer
Explanation
  1. Cash rebates are an obligation to return cash, so they reduce the net amount paid by the customer. The company estimates the rebates customers will take, reduces revenue by that estimate in the period revenue is recognized, and recognizes a liability for the estimated rebate, similar to a refundable deposit.
  2. Premiums obligate the company to provide noncash items and are treated as separate performance obligations. A portion of the original sales price is allocated to the premium (based on relative stand-alone selling prices), recorded as deferred revenue, and recognized as revenue when the premium is delivered. No contingent liability is recognized.
  3. Coupons that aren't offered as part of a sales contract don't involve revenue recognition. Issuing them creates a contingent liability recognized in the period the coupons are issued. In practice, firms either recognize the entire estimated redemption expense when the coupons are issued or record the expense when the coupons are redeemed. The difference isn't usually material.

Explanation
  1. Disclose only. A loss that is reasonably possible isn't accrued. The note describes the contingency and says an estimate can't be made.
  2. Accrue and disclose. Probable and reasonably estimable. (In practice, companies accrue litigation losses rarely, usually after a settlement has been reached or negotiations are substantially completed, which is the situation here.)
  3. Disclose only. Even after a judgment has been issued, a firm need not accrue if it is appealing and a loss isn't probable.

  1. No. A 55% chance isn't “probable” as that term is typically used in U.S. GAAP. The company would instead disclose the contingency (it's reasonably possible).
  2. Yes. IFRS defines “probable” as more likely than not (greater than 50%), a lower threshold.
