FIFO, LIFO, and Average Cost

Example 1 — FIFO (Periodic)

Balsam Gap Outfitters has the following activity for its one product:

UnitsUnit CostTotal Cost
Beginning inventory400$8.00$3,200
Purchase 1200$9.00$1,800
Purchase 2300$10.00$3,000
Available for sale900$8,000

700 units were sold, leaving 200 units on hand. Using FIFO and a periodic system, what is cost of goods sold?

$
Solution

FIFO ending inventory = the 200 units on hand are the most recently purchased, from Purchase 2: 200 × $10.00 = $2,000.
COGS = $8,000 − $2,000 = $6,000

Example 2 — LIFO (Periodic)

Using the same Balsam Gap Outfitters data above, what is cost of goods sold under LIFO (periodic)?

$
Solution

LIFO ending inventory = the 200 units on hand are assumed to be the oldest, from beginning inventory: 200 × $8.00 = $1,600.
COGS = $8,000 − $1,600 = $6,400

Example 3 — FIFO (Perpetual)

Trailhead Goods Co. has the following activity for its one product: beginning inventory of 200 units at $4.00, then a sale of 120 units, then a purchase of 150 units at $5.00, then a sale of 100 units (350 units available, 220 sold, 130 remain).

Using FIFO and a perpetual system, what is cost of goods sold?

$
Solution

Sale of 120: FIFO takes the oldest layer first — all 120 from beginning inventory @ $4.00 = $480. (80 units @ $4.00 remain.)
Purchase of 150 @ $5.00 is added.
Sale of 100: oldest layer first again — the remaining 80 @ $4.00 ($320) plus 20 @ $5.00 ($100) = $420.

COGS = $480 + $420 = $900

Note: if you worked this as a periodic problem instead — ignoring when the sales happened and just asking which 130 units are left at year-end — you'd get the exact same $900. FIFO always gives the same answer under perpetual or periodic, because "the oldest costs" doesn't depend on when during the year you check.

Example 4 — LIFO (Perpetual)

Same Trailhead Goods Co. facts as Example 3. This time, using LIFO and a perpetual system, what is cost of goods sold?

$
Solution

Sale of 120: only layer available is beginning inventory @ $4.00, so all 120 come from there = $480. (80 units @ $4.00 remain.)
Purchase of 150 @ $5.00 is added.
Sale of 100: LIFO takes the most recent layer first — the $5.00 layer — so 100 × $5.00 = $500.

COGS = $480 + $500 = $980

Unlike FIFO, this is not the same number you'd get under periodic LIFO. Applied at year-end as one pool, periodic LIFO would assume the 220 units sold came from the most recent total purchases first (all 150 @ $5.00, plus 70 more from the $4.00 layer), giving COGS of $1,030 instead of $980. Same facts, two different LIFO answers — purely a timing issue.

Example 5 — Weighted-Average (Periodic)

Sourwood Trading Co. has the following activity for its one product:

UnitsUnit CostTotal Cost
Beginning inventory300$6.00$1,800
Purchase 1200$7.00$1,400
Purchase 2500$7.60$3,800
Available for sale1,000$7,000

750 units were sold, leaving 250 units on hand. Using weighted-average cost and a periodic system, what is cost of goods sold?

$
Solution

Weighted-average unit cost = $7,000 ÷ 1,000 units = $7.00.
COGS = 750 × $7.00 = $5,250 (and ending inventory = 250 × $7.00 = $1,750, which checks out: $5,250 + $1,750 = $7,000).

Choosing an Inventory Method — Income Effects

Example 1 — Rising Costs

Using the Balsam Gap Outfitters data from Section 1 (costs rose from $8 to $9 to $10 during the period), how much lower would taxable income be under LIFO than under FIFO?

$
Solution

LIFO COGS ($6,400) − FIFO COGS ($6,000) = $400 lower income under LIFO. Because costs were rising, LIFO's higher COGS shrinks reported (and taxable) income relative to FIFO.

Example 2 — Falling Costs

Now suppose Balsam Gap Outfitters' costs had instead been falling during the year:

UnitsUnit CostTotal Cost
Beginning inventory400$10.00$4,000
Purchase 1200$9.00$1,800
Purchase 2300$8.00$2,400
Available for sale900$8,200

Again, 700 units are sold and 200 remain. What is LIFO's cost of goods sold now?

$
Solution

LIFO ending inventory = 200 units from the oldest layer (beginning inventory @ $10.00) = $2,000.
COGS = $8,200 − $2,000 = $6,200

Compare this to FIFO: ending inventory would be 200 units from the most recent layer (Purchase 2 @ $8.00) = $1,600, so FIFO COGS = $6,600. This time LIFO's COGS ($6,200) is lower than FIFO's ($6,600) — the direction reverses when costs fall.
