Key idea: Cash discounts and expected returns both mean a company may not collect the full invoice price. The gross method records a sale at full price and recognizes a discount only when one is taken; the net method records the sale net of the discount from the start. For returns, a refund liability keeps the company from recognizing revenue it expects to give back.

Sales Returns and the Refund Liability

The company used in Problems 8–10

Tuckasegee Kayak Manufacturing builds whitewater and touring kayaks and lets dealers return merchandise for credit. All sales are on account, and cost of goods sold is 70% of the selling price.

  • Refund liability, January 1, 2026: $300,000 (with a related Estimated inventory returns balance of $210,000)
  • Sales on account during 2026: $9,600,000
  • Actual returns during 2026: $408,000, of which $228,000 were from sales made before 2026 and $180,000 were from 2026 sales
