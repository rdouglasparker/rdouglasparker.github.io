Self-Constructed Assets: Interest and Overhead

A company sometimes builds an asset for its own use, often acting as general contractor and hiring subcontractors. Two questions make this harder than buying: how much overhead to assign to the project, and what to do about interest incurred while construction is underway.

Overhead

One view charges the project only with incremental overhead, meaning costs that exist only because the company decided to build. The other assigns overhead to the project using the same cost driver applied to normal production (for example, labor hours). The second, the full-cost approach, is the generally accepted method.

Capitalizing interest

Until a self-constructed asset is ready, it is not yet producing anything, so the interest on money borrowed to build it is treated as part of its cost. Key rules:

  • Qualifying assets: assets built for the company's own use, or discrete projects built for sale or lease. Routine, repetitive inventory production and assets already in use do not qualify.
  • Capitalization period: begins when construction starts, spending has begun, and interest is being incurred. It ends when the asset is substantially complete and ready, or when interest stops.
  • Amount: only interest that could have been avoided if the spending had not been made. Interest must be actual, not imputed.

The calculation has three steps:

  1. Find the weighted-average accumulated expenditures for the period by weighting each payment by the fraction of the period it was outstanding.
  2. Apply interest rates to that average. Use the specific interest method (the rate on a construction loan) up to the amount of that loan, then the weighted-average rate on other debt for the rest.
  3. Capitalize the lesser of calculated interest and actual interest incurred. Interest not capitalized is expensed.

Check yourself

In 2026 Sapphire Valley Foods began a cold-storage facility. It spent $1,800,000 on January 1, $3,600,000 on April 30, $3,000,000 on July 31, and $2,400,000 on November 30. It took a $3,000,000, 11% construction loan on January 1. Its other debt of $18,000,000 carries a weighted-average rate of 7%. How much interest is capitalized in 2026 using the specific interest method?

Weighted-average expenditures = ($1,800,000 × 12/12) + ($3,600,000 × 8/12) + ($3,000,000 × 5/12) + ($2,400,000 × 1/12) = $5,650,000.
Capitalized interest = ($3,000,000 × 11%) + ($2,650,000 × 7%) = $330,000 + $185,500 = $515,500.
Actual interest ($1,590,000) is higher, so the full $515,500 is capitalized.

Research and Development and Related Costs

Companies spend heavily on R&D hoping for future payoffs, but it is hard to know which projects will succeed, how large the benefits will be, or in which periods they will occur. Because of this uncertainty, U.S. GAAP takes the conservative route: R&D costs are expensed as incurred.

TermMeaning
ResearchPlanned investigation aimed at discovering new knowledge that may lead to a new or improved product, service, process, or technique
DevelopmentTurning research findings into a plan or design for a new or improved product or process

R&D costs include labor for R&D staff, materials consumed, services bought from others, and a reasonable share of indirect costs. The treatment of assets bought for R&D depends on how they will be used:

  • An asset that serves only one R&D project is expensed immediately, even though it lasts longer.
  • An asset with an alternative future use is capitalized and depreciated; only the depreciation for the periods used in R&D is R&D expense.

R&D ends where commercial production begins. Costs after that point, such as routine quality control and ongoing refinements, are not R&D. They are expensed or included in inventory cost.

Generally R&DGenerally not R&D
Laboratory work to discover new knowledgeEngineering follow-through early in commercial production
Finding applications for research resultsQuality control and routine testing during production
Designing, building, and testing prototypesRoutine efforts to improve an existing product
Changing the design or formulation of a product or processAdapting an existing product to a specific customer as part of ongoing business

IFRS difference: IFRS separates research from development. Research is expensed as incurred, but development costs that meet specified criteria can be capitalized as an intangible asset. Under both U.S. GAAP and IFRS, the direct costs of securing a patent (legal and filing fees) are capitalized.

Software development

For software to be sold, leased, or marketed, the dividing line is technological feasibility, reached when planning, design, coding, and testing confirm the product can meet its specifications. Costs before that point are expensed like other R&D. Costs after feasibility but before release to customers are capitalized. After release, costs such as maintenance and support are expensed (but not as R&D). For software developed for internal use, including website development, costs in the preliminary planning stage are expensed and costs in the application-development stage are capitalized.

Cloud computing arrangements

A cloud arrangement is treated as a purchased intangible only if both conditions hold: the customer has a contractual right to take possession of the software without significant penalty, and the customer could run it on its own hardware or with an unrelated vendor. If either is missing, it is a service contract and the fees are expensed as incurred.

R&D performed for others

Immediate expensing does not apply to a company that does research for clients under contract. Its costs are carried as inventory until the project is finished, because the benefit is the contract revenue that will be recognized.

R&D acquired in a business combination

  • Developed technology (already technologically feasible) is capitalized at fair value and amortized as a finite-life intangible.
  • In-process R&D is capitalized at fair value as an indefinite-life intangible until the project succeeds or fails.
  • R&D spending by the acquirer after the acquisition is expensed as incurred.

Start-up costs

The costs of launching a new product, service, territory, or customer relationship, and organization costs such as incorporation legal fees, are expensed in the period incurred rather than capitalized.

Appendix: Oil and Gas Exploration Costs

Companies may choose either of two accepted methods, and they must disclose which one they use.

  • Successful efforts: costs of wells that turn out to be dry holes are expensed in the period incurred. Only the cost of successful wells is capitalized and later depleted.
  • Full cost: all exploration costs within a large geographic area are capitalized and expensed gradually as production from the successful wells occurs.
Example — Copperhead Energy drills eight exploratory wells in 2026

Each well cost $1,500,000. Six were dry holes and two produced.

Total cost = 8 × $1,500,000 = $12,000,000.
Successful efforts: capitalize 2 × $1,500,000 = $3,000,000 and expense 6 × $1,500,000 = $9,000,000.
Full cost: capitalize the entire $12,000,000; no exploration expense this year.
