A puzzle to start

Two companies have the same equipment, the same sales, and the same costs. One has spent $2 million developing a new drug formula and the other has not. Which company reports the larger asset on its balance sheet? Under U.S. GAAP, neither: the formula’s development cost is expensed, even though the drug may be worth billions.

Intangible assets are long-lived assets that have no physical substance but have value because of the rights or advantages they give. Patents, copyrights, trademarks, franchises, and goodwill are examples. They are recorded at cost when acquired, just like PP&E. The big difference is that most intangibles a company creates itself are not recorded at all.

Where these rules live: the FASB Codification

Intangibles other than goodwill are in ASC 350-30. Goodwill is in ASC 350-20, and its measurement in a business combination is in ASC 805. Research and development is in ASC 730, software costs in ASC 985-20 (software to be sold) and ASC 350-40 (internal-use software), and start-up costs in ASC 720-15.

In the FASB Codification

  • ASC 350-30: an acquired intangible asset is recorded at cost; costs to develop internally are generally expensed.
  • ASC 805-30: goodwill is the excess of the consideration transferred over the fair value of the identifiable net assets acquired.
  • ASC 730-10: research and development costs are charged to expense when incurred.
  • ASC 985-20-25: costs of software to be sold are expensed until technological feasibility is established, then capitalized.
  • ASC 720-15-25-1: start-up costs are expensed as incurred.

Types of intangible assets

Intangibles are recorded at cost when purchased: the price plus legal fees and other costs to acquire the asset. Each type has its own characteristics.

Common intangible assets
IntangibleWhat it gives the ownerNotes on cost
PatentThe exclusive right to a product or process for 20 yearsPurchase price or registration and legal fees; costs to successfully defend it are capitalized
CopyrightThe exclusive right to a creative work, generally for the author’s life plus 70 yearsRegistration and legal costs; the creator’s own development costs are expensed
TrademarkThe exclusive right to a brand name or symbolRegistration costs, or purchase price; renewable indefinitely
FranchiseThe right to sell a product or service under the franchisor’s nameInitial fee and other costs to acquire the franchise

Those with a limited life are amortized; those with an indefinite life are not (but are tested for impairment). Both topics belong to the next chapter.

Smoky Mountain Outfitters: purchased patent and franchise

Smoky Mountain Outfitters buys a patent for $480,000, pays $20,000 in registration and legal fees, and pays a $150,000 initial franchise fee.

If the patent has a ten-year life, amortization is $500,000 ÷ 10 = $50,000 per year.

If the company must go to court and wins a suit defending the patent, the legal costs of the successful defense are added to the patent’s cost. If it loses, the costs are expensed.

Goodwill

Goodwill is the extra amount a buyer pays for a business over the fair value of its identifiable net assets. It represents loyal customers, a strong reputation, a skilled workforce, and good locations: things that cannot be separated and sold on their own.

Only purchased goodwill is recorded

If a company builds goodwill itself through years of good service, it records nothing, because the costs cannot be reliably tied to the benefit. Goodwill appears on the balance sheet only when one company buys another.

  1. Add up the fair values of the identifiable assets Include identifiable intangibles such as patents and customer lists.
  2. Subtract the fair value of the liabilities assumed The result is the fair value of net identifiable assets.
  3. Compare with the price paid Goodwill = price − net identifiable assets.

Appalachian Tech buys Cataloochee Labs

Appalachian Tech pays $5,000,000 in cash for all of Cataloochee Labs. Fair values: receivables $400,000, equipment $2,800,000, patents $1,500,000, inventories $1,500,000. Liabilities assumed: $2,100,000.

Net identifiable assets = $6,200,000 − $2,100,000 = $4,100,000. Goodwill = $5,000,000 − $4,100,000 = $900,000.

A bargain purchase

If the price is less than the fair value of net identifiable assets, there is no goodwill. The buyer first rechecks its valuations, and then recognizes the difference as a gain.

A bargain purchase

Suppose the price had been only $3,600,000.

Gain = $4,100,000 − $3,600,000 = $500,000. The entry is the same, except that Goodwill is replaced with a credit to Gain on bargain purchase of $500,000.

Research and development

Companies like pharmaceutical firms and automakers spend heavily on research (searching for new knowledge) and development (applying it to a plan or design for a new product or process). Under U.S. GAAP, all R&D costs are expensed as incurred. The reason: future benefits are too uncertain to measure, and no direct link exists between a given outlay and a given future benefit.

R&D costs: what is and is not included
Included in R&D (expensed)Not R&D
Salaries of research and development staffRoutine quality control and testing
Materials and equipment used in R&DRoutine modifications of existing products
Allocated overhead and depreciation of R&D equipmentMarket research and promotion
Costs of R&D done by others under contractLegal work to patent an invention (capitalized as a patent)

Equipment and facilities used in R&D

If equipment can be used only for a specific R&D project, its cost is expensed immediately. If it has alternative future uses, it is capitalized and its depreciation is R&D expense.

Highlands Pharmaceuticals: lab equipment

Highlands buys $300,000 of lab equipment with an 8-year life. It can be used for many projects.

Capitalize the equipment. Each year, depreciation of $300,000 ÷ 8 = $37,500 is recorded as R&D expense.

Legal costs for a patent are not R&D

Once an invention is complete, the cost of registering and defending a patent is capitalized, even though the research that led to it is expensed. A patent’s balance sheet value is therefore often much lower than its economic value.

Software, cloud computing, and other special cases

Software to be sold, leased, or marketed

Costs are expensed as R&D until technological feasibility is established (generally when a working model or detailed program design exists). After that point, production costs are capitalized until the product is available for sale.

Cullowhee Software

Cullowhee Software spends $1,000,000 developing a new accounting package. $640,000 was spent before technological feasibility.

Expense $640,000 as R&D. Capitalize $360,000 as software development costs.

Software for internal use

Internal-use software has three stages: preliminary project (expense), application development (capitalize), and post-implementation / operation (expense training and maintenance).

Cloud computing arrangements

Costs to implement a hosting arrangement that is a service contract are capitalized and amortized over the term of the arrangement, but they are presented as a prepaid asset, not as an intangible or PP&E.

Other special cases

  • R&D done for others under contract: not the company’s own R&D; costs are matched to the contract revenue.
  • Acquired in-process R&D: capitalized when acquired in a business combination (although internal R&D is expensed).
  • Start-up and organization costs: expensed as incurred.
  • Advertising: expensed (usually as incurred).
  • Internally developed brand names and customer lists: not recorded.

IFRS differences

IFRS (IAS 38) distinguishes research from development. Research is expensed. Development costs are capitalized when the company can demonstrate technical feasibility, its intent and ability to complete and use or sell the asset, how it will generate future benefits, and the availability of resources. This makes IFRS companies report larger intangible assets and, usually, higher early profits than U.S. GAAP companies.

Common mistakes

  • Capitalizing R&D costs. U.S. GAAP expenses them; only the legal costs of registering a patent are capitalized.
  • Recording self-created goodwill. Only purchased goodwill is an asset.
  • Putting goodwill on a bargain purchase. There is a gain instead.
  • Capitalizing software costs from the first day. Costs before technological feasibility are expensed.
  • Expensing all equipment used in R&D. Equipment with alternative future uses is capitalized.
  • Treating IFRS and U.S. GAAP development costs the same. IFRS allows capitalization once criteria are met.

Practice with answers

Try each problem on paper, then open the answer to check your work.

Problem 1: Goodwill

A company pays $2,400,000 for a business whose identifiable assets have fair values of $2,900,000 and whose liabilities are $900,000. What is recorded as goodwill?

Show the answer

Net identifiable assets = $2,000,000. Goodwill = $2,400,000 − $2,000,000 = $400,000.

Problem 2: Bargain purchase

If the company in the previous problem had paid $1,850,000, what would be recorded?

Show the answer

No goodwill. The $150,000 excess of net assets over price is a gain on bargain purchase.

Problem 3: R&D classification

Classify: (a) salaries of engineers designing a prototype; (b) a $40,000 legal fee to register the resulting patent; (c) $25,000 of market research; (d) a test machine that can be used only for this project, $90,000.

Show the answer

(a) R&D expense. (b) Capitalize as patent. (c) Not R&D; expense as marketing. (d) R&D expense in full because it has no alternative use.

Problem 4: Software

A firm spends $700,000 on software to be sold; $450,000 was before technological feasibility. What is expensed and what is capitalized?

Show the answer

Expense $450,000 as R&D; capitalize $250,000.

Key terms

Intangible asset
A long-lived asset with no physical substance whose value comes from the rights it conveys.
Patent
An exclusive right to a product or process granted for 20 years.
Copyright
An exclusive right to a creative work.
Trademark
An exclusive right to a name or symbol.
Franchise
A contractual right to sell a product or service under another’s name.
Goodwill
The excess of the price paid for a business over the fair value of its identifiable net assets.
Bargain purchase
A purchase at a price below the fair value of net identifiable assets, producing a gain.
Research
Planned search for new knowledge.
Development
Applying research findings to a plan or design for a new product or process.
Technological feasibility
The point at which software costs begin to be capitalized.
