Nantahala Digital

Identifying performance obligations and allocating a bundled transaction price under ASC 606.

Rounding convention: when allocating the transaction price, divide each performance obligation's standalone selling price (or expected value, for a material right) by the total standalone selling price, and round that percentage to two decimal places. Multiply the package price by that rounded percentage, then round the resulting dollar allocation to the nearest cent. When you scale a per-unit amount up to the full quantity sold, small rounding differences of a few hundred dollars on multi-million-dollar totals are expected and are not an error.
Requirements 1 & 2

Performance obligations and allocation

Nantahala Digital sells a tablet computer called the Trailrunner. The $875 sales price of a Trailrunner Package includes: one Trailrunner tablet; a six-month limited warranty guaranteeing that Nantahala will cover repair or replacement costs for defective products for up to six months; a coupon to purchase a Nantahala PageFinder e-reader for $180, a price that represents a 50% discount from the regular PageFinder price of $360 (Nantahala expects 25% of these coupons to be redeemed); and a coupon to purchase a one-year extended warranty for $70 — the same $70 price customers can pay for the extended warranty at other times as well (Nantahala estimates 45% of customers will purchase an extended warranty).

Nantahala does not sell the Trailrunner without the limited warranty, the option to purchase a PageFinder, and the option to purchase an extended warranty, but estimates that if it did so, a Trailrunner alone would sell for $855. All Trailrunner sales are made in cash.
