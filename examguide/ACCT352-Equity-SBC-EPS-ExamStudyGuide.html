Key Theory

Key idea: Most multiple-choice questions test which account is used, which date triggers an entry, or which amount is measured.

Concept notes: the rules behind the multiple choice

Quick-reference rules
TopicRule to know
Treasury stock (cost method)Debit Treasury stock at cost. It is contra-equity, the shares are not outstanding, and there is never a gain or loss in income.
Shares issued for noncash itemsRecord at the fair value of the shares or of the asset received, whichever is more clearly evident.
Share issue costsReduce the net proceeds, so they reduce paid-in capital. They are not an expense.
Cash dividend datesDeclaration: debit Retained earnings, credit Dividends payable. Date of record and ex-dividend date: no entry. Payment: debit Dividends payable, credit Cash.
Cumulative preferredSkipped dividends become dividends in arrears: disclosed, not a liability, and paid before any common dividend.
Stock dividend vs. splitA small stock dividend moves fair value from retained earnings to paid-in capital. A split needs no entry. Total equity never changes.
Share-based compensationMeasure fair value on the grant date; expense it over the service period; make no entry on the grant date; later price changes do not matter.
Expired optionsDo not reverse expense; rename the paid-in capital.
EPS and stock dividendsRestate shares outstanding before a stock dividend or split as if it happened at the start of the year.
Antidilutive securitiesAnything that would raise EPS is excluded, including options with an exercise price above the average market price.

Study tip: For a theory question, name the account or amount first (for example, “fair value out of retained earnings”), then read the choices. Wrong answers usually swap one account or one date.

Issuing Shares, Treasury Stock, and Dividends

Key idea: Equity transactions with owners never create gains or losses in income; they move amounts between retained earnings and paid-in capital.

Concept notes: issuing shares, treasury stock, and dividends

Issuing shares

Credit the stock account for shares × par; the rest of the proceeds is paid-in capital—excess of par. For no-par stock with no stated value, all proceeds go to the stock account. Shares are recorded at what was received, so par value only decides how the credit is split.

Treasury stock (cost method)

Treasury stock events
EventEntry
Buy back sharesDebit Treasury stock for the cost; credit Cash.
Resell above costDebit Cash; credit Treasury stock at cost; credit Paid-in capital—share repurchase for the excess.
Resell below costDebit Cash; credit Treasury stock at cost; debit Paid-in capital—share repurchase up to its balance, then Retained earnings.

Cash and preferred dividends

Dividends are paid only on outstanding shares. For cumulative preferred, pay the annual preferred dividend plus any arrears first; common shareholders receive the rest. A noncumulative preferred never carries arrears.

Property dividends

First adjust the distributed asset to its fair value and recognize the gain or loss; then debit Retained earnings for the fair value and credit Property dividends payable.

Stock dividends and splits

Choosing the entry
EventEntry
Small stock dividend (under about 20% to 25%)Debit Retained earnings at fair value of the new shares; credit Common stock at par and Paid-in capital—excess of par for the rest.
Large stock dividend or a split effected as a dividendMove only the par value of the new shares into the stock account.
True stock splitNo entry. Par falls and the number of shares rises.

Study tip: Before a stock dividend, count the shares outstanding at that date. Treasury shares do not receive stock dividends or cash dividends.

Share-Based Compensation

Key idea: Measure the award once at grant-date fair value, expense it over the service period, and true up the estimate as vesting becomes known.

Concept notes: stock options and restricted stock units

Measuring and expensing the award

Measure the award at fair value on the grant date and spread it over the service (vesting) period. Each year: debit Compensation expense; credit Paid-in capital—stock options (or restricted stock for RSUs). Nothing is recorded on the grant date, and later share price changes do not change the total for an award settled in shares.

Total compensation = options granted × fair value per option × (1 − estimated forfeiture rate)

Changing the forfeiture estimate

A change in the estimate is a cumulative catch-up in the year of the change: compute the revised total, multiply by the fraction of the service period completed, and subtract what has already been expensed. When the final vesting count is known, true up the same way.

Exercise, expiration, and RSUs

What happens later
EventEntry
Options exercisedDebit Cash (options × exercise price) and Paid-in capital—stock options (options × fair value); credit Common stock at par and Paid-in capital—excess of par for the rest. The market price on the exercise date does not matter.
Vested options expireNo reversal of expense. Rename the balance, for example to Paid-in capital—expired stock options.
RSUs forfeitedReverse the expense already recorded for the forfeited units (debit Paid-in capital—restricted stock, credit Compensation expense).
RSUs vestDebit Paid-in capital—restricted stock; credit Common stock at par and Paid-in capital—excess of par.

Study tip: Write the cumulative expense you need at the end of each year, then subtract what is already recorded. The yearly entry is the difference.

Basic and Diluted Earnings per Share

Key idea: Basic EPS divides earnings available to common shareholders by weighted shares; diluted EPS adds each potential common share that lowers EPS.

Concept notes: basic and diluted earnings per share

Basic EPS

Basic EPS = (net income − preferred dividends) ÷ weighted-average common shares

  • Weight shares sold or bought back by the fraction of the year they were outstanding.
  • Restate shares outstanding before a stock dividend or split as if it happened on January 1; do not restate shares sold or bought after it.
  • Subtract cumulative preferred dividends whether or not declared; for noncumulative, subtract only declared dividends. Common dividends never matter.

Diluted EPS

Methods for potential common shares
SecurityMethodWhat changes
Options, warrantsTreasury stock methodAdd shares; subtract shares the proceeds would buy at the average market price. The numerator does not change.
Convertible bondsIf-convertedAdd back interest net of tax; add the conversion shares.
Convertible preferredIf-convertedDo not subtract its dividends (no tax effect); add the conversion shares.
  • An option or warrant with an exercise price above the average market price is antidilutive and ignored.
  • Add options first, then add convertibles from the lowest incremental effect upward, including each only if its incremental effect (numerator effect ÷ shares added) is below the EPS computed so far.

Study tip: Build diluted EPS in a three-column table (numerator, denominator, EPS). Each row adds one security, and you stop when the next one would raise EPS.
