Key idea: Under the allowance method, a company estimates the receivables it won't collect and records that estimate as bad debt expense in the same period as the related sales. An income statement approach estimates the expense directly (a percentage of credit sales); a balance sheet approach estimates the allowance balance that should remain (a percentage of receivables, or an aging schedule) and records whatever expense gets it there.

Under the allowance method, a write-off never touches Bad debt expense. The expense was already estimated earlier; a write-off just confirms that a specific piece of the estimate has become real, so it moves out of the allowance and off accounts receivable.

A recovery runs the write-off in reverse: first reinstate the receivable, then record its collection like any other cash receipt.
