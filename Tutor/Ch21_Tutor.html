The one question to ask

  • Did cash change hands?
    If not, is it a noncash investing or financing event to disclose?
  • ↓
  • Selling goods or paying expenses → operating
    Buying or selling long-term assets → investing
    Raising or repaying capital → financing
  • ↓
  • Operating, direct method: start with the income statement item and adjust for the related balance sheet accounts

Rules that never bend

  1. Gains and losses are never cash flows. The sale proceeds are investing.
  2. Depreciation and amortization are noncash. Add them back in the reconciliation.
  3. Interest paid is operating. Dividends paid are financing.
  4. Operating assets up uses cash. Operating liabilities up keeps cash.
  5. The direct and indirect methods always give the same operating total.
Direct method
Reports each operating cash receipt and payment.
Indirect method
Starts with net income and adjusts it to operating cash flow.
Cash equivalent
A short-term, highly liquid investment, such as a Treasury bill, treated as cash.
Noncash activity
A significant investing or financing event with no cash, disclosed separately.
